# Building Maps in Code

How [[Cliffside]]'s gym is generated by `tools/style-gym.luau`, a script run in Studio **Edit** mode. Rebuilding from code makes the map repeatable, reviewable in git and easy to tweak. See also [[Lighting]] and [[Studio Gotchas]].

## The basic pattern
- Everything generated goes under one model (`GeneratedGym`). The script destroys the old one first, so reruns are safe.
- Assert you're in Edit mode: `assert(not RunService:IsRunning(), "Run in Edit mode")`.
- Write one `place(name, size, cframe, color, parent, options)` helper. It sets `Anchored`, `CanTouch = false`, collision and query flags, material and smooth surfaces in one spot.
- Work in a **local frame**. Cliffside builds everything relative to a `LayoutRoot` part (`origin * CFrame.new(x, y, z)`), so moving that one part moves the whole map.
- Group parts into folders by purpose (Room, Wall, Props, Training, Lights).

## CFrame recipes
- `CFrame.lookAt(pos, target)` points a part's **-Z** (its front) at the target.
- Yaw a "kit" of furniture with `CFrame.Angles(0, yaw, 0)`. With front = local -Z, yaw `0` faces -Z, `math.pi` faces +Z, `-math.pi/2` faces +X and `math.pi/2` faces -X.
- A positive rotation about X tips the part's top toward its +Z (back). To lean a wall's top toward the room, rotate by a **negative** angle.
- `origin:ToObjectSpace(cf)` and `origin:PointToObjectSpace(p)` convert world positions back into the local frame.

## Shapes from primitives
- **Cylinders** run along the part's **X axis**. For an upright cylinder, size it `(height, d, d)` and rotate `CFrame.Angles(0, 0, math.pi / 2)`.
- **Ellipsoids:** a `Ball` part is always a sphere. Put a `SpecialMesh` with `MeshType = Sphere` in a normal part and it stretches to the part's size. Cliffside's resin holds, plants and exercise balls are built this way.
- **Any triangle:** two `WedgePart`s back to back (the `tri(a, b, c)` helper). Triangles build volumes (pyramids bolted to the wall) and flat painted shapes.
- **Text on a surface:** a transparent part with a `SurfaceGui` and a `TextLabel`. `Face = Back` shows on the part's +Z side and `Front` on -Z. `LightInfluence = 0` keeps text bright, and 1 lets it take scene lighting.
- **Meshes from Blender:** import them once into `ServerStorage`, then `:Clone()` a template MeshPart per use and scale it. Keep the gameplay part (the collider or target) separate from the visual mesh skin.

## Making it look like a real place
- Look at reference photos first: real bouldering gyms have flat walls with dense bolt holes, coloured volumes, thick grey mats, clean floors and distinct zones.
- **Zones** make a hall feel real: a climbing wall, a front desk by the entrance, cubbies and lockers, a lounge or cafe, a warm-up area and a training corner.
- Fill empty floor with purpose, not clutter. Keep walkways clear and fall zones (mats) free of furniture and columns.
- Use a restrained palette: white walls, one accent colour (navy), wood, grey mats, plus the hold colours.
- Avoid pure white everywhere. Add a darker floor, a painted lower-wall band with a stripe, and darker mats so things separate.
- **Everything that looks interactive should be interactive.** Decorative holds that can't be climbed confused the player. Every hold in the gym now belongs to a real route.

## Gotchas hit while building
- The default `Baseplate` sat 0.5 studs **above** the custom floor and hid it, along with anything placed on it. Move it under the floor in the build script.
- Overlapping coplanar surfaces z-fight. Offset decals and seams by 0.003+ studs, or make them slightly thicker.
- A `Random.new(seed)` per feature keeps layouts stable when you edit one section.
- Count parts after a build (`GetDescendants`, filter `BasePart`) to keep an eye on the budget.

# Characters and Animation

What [[Cliffside]] learned about rigs, procedural poses and dressing a character. See also [[Game Architecture]].

## Rigs
- An R15 character may use **`AnimationConstraint`** joints (attachments `Attachment0`/`Attachment1`), not just `Motor6D` (`C0`/`C1`, `Part0`). Support both when reading joints.
- **Measure** the rig instead of guessing. Sum the joint-to-joint distances (shoulder to elbow to wrist to hand) from the authored attachments of the neutral StarterCharacter. Cliffside's arm reach is 1.811 studs and leg reach 1.900. Gameplay reach, route spacing and tests all use those numbers.
- Measure from the authored `StarterCharacter`, never the animated client pose, so server and client agree.

## Procedural pose and IK
- The default `Animate` script fights procedural poses. Stop or disable it while your code owns the pose, and restore it when walking resumes.
- Pick one owner per joint per frame. Animator plus IK plus physics all writing the same joint caused shaking and knee flicker.
- Order matters: prepare in `PreAnimation`, apply in `PreSimulation`. A debug camera must bind after the game camera, or it gets overwritten.
- Smooth targets with a critically damped follow (a smooth time of about 0.06–0.1 s). Short phases (reach, then grab) read better than one long lerp.
- Anchor the root during kinematic motion (climbing, scripted landings) and release it when the player walks again.

## Dressing the character
- `BodyColors` sets skin and outfit colours without new meshes.
- Accessories such as a harness, straps and chalk bag are simple parts **welded** to body parts, with `Massless = true` and `CanCollide = false`, so they don't change physics.
- Keep the dressing in a script (`tools/style-climber.luau`) so it can be rerun after rig changes.

## Reading the player's feel
- "It looks like I can reach it but I can't" means visuals and gameplay disagree. Cliffside fixed it by moving the climber's hips closer to the wall (`ClimbDepth`), which increased in-plane reach to match the arms on screen.
- Snappier and bouncier movement came from tuning numbers (stiffness, damping, dyno lift and carry, catch damping), not new code. Keep them in `Config`.

# Game Architecture

How [[Cliffside]] splits code between shared simulation, server authority and client presentation. The pattern carries over to any movement-heavy multiplayer game. See also [[Luau Coding]] and [[Testing]].

## Folder layout (mirrors Roblox services)
- `ReplicatedStorage/Shared`: pure simulation modules both sides run (Motion, Support, Actions, Fatigue, Fall, Start, Config, Types). No instances are created and no UI lives here.
- `ServerScriptService/Server`: authority (ClimbService owns live state, AttemptService owns results, ActionValidator rejects bad input).
- `StarterPlayer/StarterPlayerScripts/Client`: input, prediction, camera, pose/IK, HUD and effects.
- `.server.luau` files are Scripts, `.client.luau` files are LocalScripts and anything else is a ModuleScript (Rojo naming).

## Server authority + client prediction
- The client sends **intent** (grip pressed, weight direction, start held). The server validates it, steps the same shared simulation and owns the result: contacts, stamina, completion.
- The client runs the same shared step for instant feedback, then reconciles to the server's snapshots (sent about 20 times a second as a `StateSnapshot` RemoteEvent).
- Timed actions (like "hold E for 0.4 s to start") are counted on the **server's** fixed clock. The client can't send its own elapsed time.
- `ActionValidator` gives each input a sequence number and generation, rate-limits it, and rejects stale generations and client-supplied durations.
- Inputs use keepalives and leases, so a held key that stops arriving is treated as released.

## Simulation design
- **Fixed step** (1/60 s) with a substep cap, so the same inputs give the same results on both sides and in tests.
- **Plain data state.** The state table (position, velocity, contacts, arms, status) is plain Luau, so tests can build one with `Motion.new(...)` and step it with no Roblox instances.
- Transactional changes: a solver (`Support.solve`) checks a candidate pose. If it fails, the old state is kept instead of being half-applied.
- Leave float tolerance in geometric checks (Cliffside lands 1e-5 inside a limb's reach). Otherwise rounding reads as overextension.
- Tuning lives in `Config`, including values measured from the real rig. Arm and leg length are read from the StarterCharacter's joint attachments, not guessed.

## Maps as data
- Routes are just tagged parts. Each hold has attributes (`HoldId`, `Kind`, `Role`, `RouteId`, `GripType`) and a `Grip` attachment for the exact gameplay point. A registry reads them at runtime.
- The map builder sets those attributes, so adding a route is just data. The route-climbing bot test checks that every authored route can actually be finished.
- Keep gameplay geometry (the attachment point and collider) separate from visuals (mesh skin, roll, colour). Visual tweaks then can't break gameplay.

## Limits worth knowing up front
- Cliffside's climbing is on **one flat plane** (a global `Config.Origin` and `RootZ`). Climbing on side walls or boulders needs per-route wall frames, which is a real engine change. Decide on single or multi-plane early, because every other system assumes it.

# Lighting

What made [[Cliffside]]'s gym go from flat and washed out to having depth. Lighting values live in the map build script ([[Building Maps in Code]]), so they're versioned with the map.

## Why scenes look flat
- The **sun was behind the wall**. The wall face only got ambient light and every hold's shadow fell away from the viewer. Check `Lighting:GetSunDirection()`: for a wall facing +Z, you want the direction's Z to be **positive**, so the sun sits in front of it.
- **Ambient too bright.** High `Ambient` and `OutdoorAmbient` fill every shadow, so nothing has shape. Lower them and let the sun do the work.
- **Soft shadows everywhere.** `ShadowSoftness` around 0.25 gives crisp contact shadows under holds and volumes.

## Aiming the sun
The sun is set by `ClockTime` and `GeographicLatitude`. Search for the pair whose direction is closest to the one you want:

```lua
local target = Vector3.new(0.3, 0.78, 0.55).Unit -- front, upper right
local best, bc, bl = -2, 0, 0
for c = 6, 18, 0.25 do
	for lat = -80, 80, 5 do
		Lighting.ClockTime, Lighting.GeographicLatitude = c, lat
		local score = Lighting:GetSunDirection():Dot(target)
		if score > best then best, bc, bl = score, c, lat end
	end
end
```
Cliffside ended up with `ClockTime = 10.5` and `GeographicLatitude = 55`.

## Indoor halls
- If the roof and walls cast shadows, the sun never gets in and the interior is lit only by ambient light, which looks flat. Cliffside keeps the **shell's `CastShadow` off** so sunlight reaches the inside, while holds, volumes and furniture cast shadows.
- Ceiling LED strips: Neon parts with a downward `SurfaceLight` (shadows off) add fill and read as real fixtures.
- `Lighting.Technology` can't even be read from plugin or command-bar scripts (it needs RobloxScript capability). Set it in the Properties panel.

## Values that worked (Cliffside v3)
- Brightness 2.2, ExposureCompensation 0
- Ambient (98, 102, 112), OutdoorAmbient (128, 132, 142)
- ShadowSoftness 0.25, EnvironmentDiffuseScale 1, EnvironmentSpecularScale 0.5
- ColorCorrection: contrast 0.14, saturation 0.08, tint very slightly cool
- Bloom: intensity 0.25, size 24, threshold 1.8. Too much bloom makes white walls glow.

# Luau Coding

Patterns and syntax that worked well in [[Cliffside]]. See also [[Game Architecture]] and [[Testing]].

## Strict mode and types
- Put `--!strict` on the first line of every module. It catches nil access and wrong argument types before you hit Play.
- Share types from one `Types` module and `export type` them: `export type Hold = { id: string, point: Vector3, kind: string }`.
- Cast an instance you just found: `local wall = area:FindFirstChild("Wall") :: BasePart`. Pair it with an `assert` so a missing part fails loudly.
- Optional fields use `?`: `hands: number?`. Read them with a fallback: `local hands = spec.hands or HANDS`.
- Function types: `center: (number) -> number`. Functions can return several values with a typed tuple: `(Add, CFrame)`.
- Tables keyed from 0 need an explicit index type, `{ [number]: { number } }`. `{ number }` is an array from 1.
- A type checker such as luau-lsp may report many errors on dynamic `require`s and on instances that only exist in the place file. Treat that count as informational and fix what's real.

## Handy syntax
- If-expressions: `local damping = if feet then 0.65 else 1.4`.
- Compound assignment: `count += 1`, `s.velocity *= 0.6`.
- Multiple assignment for properties: `p.Anchored, p.CanCollide = true, false`.
- Generalised `for` over tables needs no `pairs`: `for id, hold in holds do`.
- String building: `"V" .. grade`. Use `string.format("%.2f", x)` for numbers.
- `math.clamp`, `math.sign`, `math.round` and `Vector3:Lerp` are all built in. `Color3:Lerp(Color3.new(), 0.3)` darkens a colour.

## Modules and requires
- A `ModuleScript` runs once per Luau VM. Later `require` calls return the cached table.
- That cache persists across edits in Studio. After changing a module, start a **fresh Play session**, or `require` a `:Clone()` of the module in the command bar.
- Keep tuning numbers in one `Config` module. Everything else, tests included, reads them from there, never as copied literals. When Cliffside raised stamina from 60 to 150 seconds, three tests broke because they had `60` hard-coded.
- Give modules `function Module.thing()` style APIs and return the table at the end.

## Determinism
- Use `Random.new(seed)` rather than `math.random` for anything generated, such as map layout and hold placement. The same seed rebuilds the same map every time, and tests stay stable.
- Run the simulation on a fixed step (Cliffside uses 1/60 s with a substep cap), not the variable frame `dt`.

## Performance habits
- Building thousands of parts in one go freezes Studio. Call `task.wait()` every ~150 parts (a `batch()` counter).
- Anchor static parts. Turn off `CanTouch`, plus `CanCollide`/`CanQuery` on anything purely visual.
- Decorative detail adds up fast: 4,000 bolt-hole dots is fine, but budget for it. Keep total parts in the low thousands per area where you can.

## Lint and format
- **StyLua** formats (`stylua src tests tools`). **Selene** lints (`selene src tests tools`). Run both before committing.
- Selene flags unused variables and shadowing, such as a local `gaps` table shadowed by a function parameter named `gaps`. Rename rather than suppress.

# Rojo Workflow

Details beyond the basics in [[Toolchain]]. See also [[Studio MCP]].

## Project file
- `default.project.json` maps folders on disk to services. Map **only scripts**, such as Shared, Server, Client, tests and tools, so Rojo leaves the map, character model and authored RemoteEvents alone.
- `"$ignoreUnknownInstances": true` on a mapped instance keeps the Studio-only children it has, such as the character's parts under a synced `Animate` script.
- Map build tools into `ServerStorage` (for example `ServerStorage.CliffsideTools.StyleGym`), so you can run them from Studio without them shipping to clients.
- Leave client-only test folders out of the server test mapping.

## Day to day
- `rojo serve`, then press **Connect** in the Studio plugin. While connected, **edit files on disk, not in Studio**, or your Studio edits get overwritten.
- Rojo pushes only when file **content** changes. To force a re-push, add a byte, save, then remove it.
- Restarting Studio or the server means pressing Connect again.

## Several agents, one Studio
- Only one Rojo server can feed a Studio at a time. If two coding sessions each run `rojo serve` from different folders (git worktrees), the second takes over and the first one's code stops syncing.
- Before testing, check which folder Rojo is serving. Studio then runs that folder's code, which may not be your branch.
- One session overwriting another's synced scripts is the usual cause of "my change disappeared". Coordinate who owns Rojo and Studio, and restore by nudging the files so Rojo re-pushes.

## Toolchain pins
- Rokit pins the versions in `rokit.toml`. `rokit add` fails until the tool is trusted (`rokit trust <tool>`).
- Tools land in `~/.rokit/bin`. Add it to `PATH` in scripts that call `stylua` or `selene`.

# Studio Gotchas

- **Require cache:** after editing modules, start a fresh Play session or you'll test old code.
- **Ctrl+S may not save** when tools changed the scripts. Use Save As and check the file's timestamp.
- **Camera locks after an MCP screen capture:** set Workspace > Camera > CameraType to Fixed in the Properties panel (setting it from a script through the MCP may not stick). See [[Studio MCP]].
- **A Highlight with no Adornee** tints the whole scene. Disable the highlight before clearing its Adornee.
- **R15 rigs may use AnimationConstraint joints**, not just Motor6D.
- **The default Animate script fights procedural poses.** Disable it while you're controlling the pose.
- **The default Baseplate can cover your floor.** If your floor is lower than the Baseplate's top, the Baseplate's grid hides it and anything placed on it. Move the Baseplate under the hall.
- **`Lighting.Technology` can't be read or set from scripts** (plugin or command bar). Use the Properties panel.
- **Sun behind the wall makes everything flat.** Check `Lighting:GetSunDirection()`. See [[Lighting]].
- **Two Rojo servers fight.** Only one folder can sync into Studio at a time. See [[Rojo Workflow]].
- **Saves can land late.** Check the `.rbxl` timestamp after File > Save to File before committing.

# Studio MCP

Driving Roblox Studio from an AI coding agent through the Roblox Studio MCP plugin. These notes come from building [[Cliffside]]. See also [[Rojo Workflow]] and [[Studio Gotchas]].

## Setup
- Studio has a built-in MCP server. The client config runs `cmd.exe /c cd /d %LOCALAPPDATA%\Roblox && .\mcp.bat`.
- `list_roblox_studios` gives the id of each open Studio. Get a fresh id every session rather than reusing an old one.

## Data models
- `execute_luau` runs in one data model: **Edit** (the place you save), **Server** or **Client** (only while playing).
- Build maps and change sources in **Edit**. Run server tests in **Server** after `start_stop_play(true)`.
- `get_studio_state` tells you which data models exist right now.

## Running code safely
- Code runs on Studio's main thread, so long loops freeze it and can drop the connection. Yield with `task.wait()` every ~150 parts.
- Run a build tool by requiring a **clone** so the require cache doesn't serve stale code:
  ```lua
  local m = game.ServerStorage.CliffsideTools.StyleGym:Clone()
  local ok, result = pcall(require, m)
  m:Destroy()
  ```
- Before running synced code, check it actually synced: search its `.Source` for a string you just added.
- Return a small table of results (counts, ok and error) rather than printing.

## Screenshots
- `screen_capture` with no camera arguments captures the current view. That's the safe option, especially during Play, where it shows the player's camera.
- `screen_capture` **with** a camera position temporarily takes over the edit camera. If the capture times out, the camera can stay **Scriptable**, and the user can no longer move it.
- Setting `CameraType = Fixed` through `execute_luau` didn't stick: the plugin switched it back after each call. The fix that works is in the Properties panel (Workspace > Camera > CameraType = Fixed), toggling the MCP plugin, or reopening the place.
- So when the user is working in Studio, don't move their camera. Ask them to frame the view, or capture during Play.
- If the 3D view is black, Studio isn't rendering in the background. The window has to be in front.

## Saving
- Changes made through the MCP may not mark the place dirty. Save with **File > Save to File (As)** into `places/`, then check the file's timestamp and size before committing. Saves can land a few seconds late.

# Testing

How [[Cliffside]] tests gameplay inside Roblox Studio. See also [[Game Architecture]] and [[Studio MCP]].

## Spec modules
- Each test is a ModuleScript in `tests/` (synced to `ServerStorage.CliffsideTests`) that **returns a function**: `return function(shared, server) ... end`. The runner calls it and catches errors with `pcall`.
- Use `assert(condition, message)` with a useful message that includes the actual value: `"W hang must exhaust at about 75 s; got " .. push`.
- Return a count or a small summary table, so the runner can report what was checked.

## Running them
- Server tests run in the **Server** context of a **fresh Play session** (`tools/run-tests.luau`). Fresh matters because of the require cache.
- Client presentation tests (pose, camera, landing clearance) run separately on the client. Don't mix them into the server suite.
- Fixtures assume the original origin. The runner sets `Config.setOrigin(Vector3.zero)` and puts it back afterwards.

## What made tests reliable
- **Read tuning from `Config`, never literals.** Write `Config.StaminaSeconds / 2`, not `30`. Then changing a balance number doesn't break three unrelated tests.
- **Derive frame counts** from durations: `math.ceil(Start.HoldDuration / Config.Step - 1e-6)`.
- **A bot that plays the real map.** `RouteClimb.spec` starts each authored route, steps the real simulation with real inputs (grip, feet, W, dynos) and asserts it reaches the finish. It iterates **every** route found on the map, not a hard-coded list, so new routes are covered automatically.
- Keep tests deterministic: fixed steps, seeded randomness, explicit orders (sort keys before iterating dictionaries).
- When a fix changes geometry such as climb depth, update fixtures to use the config value (`Config.RootZ`) rather than old literals (`2.15`).

## Before you say it's done
- Format and lint (StyLua, Selene).
- Run every server suite in a fresh Play session.
- Say plainly which suites you didn't run (for example, client presentation tests).
- Screenshots from the player's view catch what tests can't. A passing test does not mean it looks right.

# Cliffside

Skill-based bouldering game. You grip holds with each hand, shift your weight, place your feet and dyno between holds, all while managing arm stamina.

**Related:** [[Mechanics]] · [[Tuning Log]] · [[Decisions]] · [[Backlog]] · [[Saves]]

## Controls
- **Left / right mouse:** grip with that hand
- **WASD:** shift weight while climbing; walk on the ground
- **Space:** hold to charge a dyno, release to launch at an aimed hold; press grip again to catch
- **Q / E:** pick a foot (Manual feet mode). **Hold E** at the start holds to begin a route
- **Left Alt:** orbit the camera. **R:** recenter it

## Where things live in the repo
- Gameplay source: `src/` (Shared = simulation, Server = authority, Client = prediction and presentation)
- Tuning values: `src/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Config.luau`
- Routes: `src/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/RouteDefinitions.luau`
- Gym builder: `tools/style-gym.luau`
- Long-form docs: `docs/HANDOFF.md`, `docs/verification/playtest-notes.md`
- Reference clips: `reference videos/`, `reference analysis/`

## The gym right now
One flat wall across the whole hall with thirteen routes that have grade tags. Navy volumes, a full-width grey crash mat, Blender resin hold meshes (Crimp, Jug, Pinch, Pocket, Sloper), plus a front desk, lounge, cafe, warm-up area and training corner.

# Decisions

Part of [[Cliffside]]. These are calls I've made on purpose. Don't undo them without a reason.

- **Dynos need a real target hold.** No free-cursor launches.
- **Keep the core-following sway.** It's looseness and follow-through, not random hip wiggle.
- **Peter flop and resurrection are removed.** All long falls use the normal roll and kip-up.
- **HUD:** thin rounded meters, stamina on the right of the character and dyno charge on the left. No bottom guidance panel.
- **Wall-clipping fix rejected.** It looked worse and was rolled back.
- **Walk-up start/restart** stays.
- **Save the actual .rbxl place** when gameplay changes. Saving only the source has caused confusion.
- **Don't publish the experience** during local development.
- **2026-10-18: bigger reach, lower walls, real overhangs.** Walls are bouldering height (about 4.5 m for the rig), every wall in the gym is climbable at its own angle (cave, slab, steep overhangs, side walls, a freestanding boulder), and overhangs use real tilted gravity. Work is in the `jolly-chaum-62be55` worktree, uncommitted.
- **2026-10-18: falls should feel dynamic.** Velocity-driven airborne pose and momentum-driven landing; the roll onto the back and Space kip-up stay.
- **2026-10-18: tall zone is top rope on auto-belays.** A fall onto the rope fails the clean ascent but you can grab back on and keep working moves; Space lowers you. Roof routes are "projects" that need upgraded stats (RouteClimb checks them with 1.5x power).
- **2026-10-18: spray-wall problems share holds.** Starts and finishes are unique per problem; the board lights holds client-side only.

# Mechanics

Part of [[Cliffside]].

## Stamina and fatigue
- Each arm has its own stamina. Loaded arms drain; unloaded arms recover, but only up to a fatigue ceiling.
- Holding **W** (pushing up) costs 2× effort (`UpwardEffortMultiplier`).
- Foot support extends how long you can hang.
- On the ground, everything refills over 8 seconds (`GroundRecoverySeconds`).

## Dynos
- Charge with Space; launch power comes from the charge (charge^1.5), not from distance to the target.
- You **must aim at a real hold** to launch. This is deliberate (see [[Decisions]]).
- When you catch a dyno, you keep 60% of the flight velocity (`CatchDamping`), so the body swings on through.

## Body
- The body is a spring under the hands, with light damping so it can swing. A/D pumps the swing (`SwingPump`).
- Sway is core-following: the legs and lower body lag behind the moving core.

## Falling
- Long falls: roll onto your back, then press Space to kip-up (1.65 s).
- Short drops: a wide crouch, then you stand up automatically.

## Feet
- Auto or Manual mode. Auto prefers to stay on its current hold, with some hysteresis.
- Leg reach preset: 2.0 studs (the measured rig is about 2.04).
